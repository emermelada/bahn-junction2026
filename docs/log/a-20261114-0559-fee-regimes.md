# Higher fees and prices together: volume collapses

**What we saw.** Every idea that adds orders loses money; drivers are the limit. The top teams move about 300 orders a week at 97% on time, so they reach more drivers in time. Our fees are the cheapest that work (1.0x long slack, 1.4x short).

**The idea.** Pay more to reach more drivers and charge more to pay for it, as whole regimes rather than single knobs.

**The test.** rc8-01..12, paired against rc4 defaults (fees 1.0x / 1.4x, ratio 1.4, backlog 15).

| Regime | Fees long / short | Ratio | Backlog | Ranked | Min on-time | Median | Mean delta | Orders a week |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| base | 1.0 / 1.4 | 1.4 | 15 | 12/12 | 92.0% | €5,763 | | 194 |
| 1 | 1.2 / 1.6 | 1.5 | 20 | 12/12 | 95.0% | €3,275 | -€2,225 | 118 |
| 2 | 1.3 / 1.7 | 1.6 | 25 | 12/12 | 97.1% | €2,131 | -€3,580 | 74 |
| 3 | 1.15 / 1.5 | 1.5 | 15 | 12/12 | 94.0% | €3,802 | -€1,465 | 132 |

**Keep or kill.** Kill. Higher fees do buy on-time share (up to 97.1%), but the higher planned cost plus the higher price fail the margin floor or lose to the competitor, so orders fall by a third to two thirds. Next: more cars per driver instead of more drivers. A two-car chain moves two cars with one driver-day; the first chain version held new cars back up to 2 hours and lost on-time share. Version 2 holds nothing: it pairs each new car with another new car or with a single still open and unbooked (withdraw and re-post as one gig).
