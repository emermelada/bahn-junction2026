# V2: capacity-first planner (`v2=true`)

## What we saw
- Board (Sat 09:00/12:00 rounds): c3 wins 264–379 orders a run; the top ten win 398–530 at 0.95–0.985 on time and EUR 35–42 an
  order. Our worst board run learned a competitor level of 0.87–0.96 (true 1.26–1.54) and declined 446 of 1,040 requests.
- c3 prices each car against a flat "load" charge per deadline day and the whole roster; dispatch opens low and raises on a ladder.
  Capacity, not price knowledge, is the limit (organisers' oracle: 16.6k but ranked 7/20).

## The idea (research/sessionA/V2_SPEC.md)
Plan driver-hours first, then price and dispatch against the plan. `src/spreadbot/planner.py`:
- Supply (a3, `supply.py`): driver-hours per (region, day). Fees (a4, `fees.py`): the flat clearing fee of a gig. Stubs until they land.
- Planner.replan every world hour: committed gigs keep their hours; each open car goes to the feasible day with the lowest
  utilisation (ties -> latest); cars on one (region, day) are chained 1–3 per driver (<= 12 h, windows, deadlines), nearest end first;
  expected future wins (request rate per region x win share x hours per car) are added; lam(region, day) = 20 EUR/h x
  (u − 0.5)/(1 − u), capped at 150.
- Price: each option at best_ratio with cost = predicted clearing fee and shadow = lam(region, planned day) x hours of the car;
  no load charge. Governor, bar and late risk unchanged.
- Dispatch: a car waits until hold_hour the evening before its planned day, then its chain is posted at the flat clearing fee with one
  insurance raise (x1.3 at 8 h before the last pickup); own_alarm escalation and the steep ladder for urgent cars stay.
