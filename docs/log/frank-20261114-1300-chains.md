## 2. Chains
**What we saw** (`lab/frank/gigcost.py`, `chainpot.py`). A two-car gig costs 0.85× the typical fees and a single 1.14×. Most cars are won hours before they are ready. With hindsight, 71% of won cars could ride in chains of ≤6 h of driving, but we chained 11%. Bundled cars are late 1–3%, singles 9%.

**Idea.** Hold a won car, unposted, for up to 8 h (posted at the latest 15 h before its pickup opens) and pair it with another held car or a fresh open single (`hold_minutes`).

**Why the first version lost on-time** (`latekind.py`, `bundlebook.py`). A third of bundles found no driver and were split late; split cars ran 16.5% late at 1.31×. Raising the bundle fee did not help (`bundle_rel`). Bookings depend on the route: 74% booked under 4 h of driving, 17% at 10–12 h; 46% when formed under 2 h before the start. Cap the chain (`bundle_max_drive`) and require lead (`bundle_min_lead`).

**The ceiling.** In hindsight, with our rules (hold ≤8 h, post ≥15 h before pickup, chain ≤5 h, lead ≥6 h), about 95 cars a week could ride in chains; we chain 84. Lifting the posting rule to 10 h gives 117, and unlimited holding gives 137, but posting later makes held singles late (t4, t5). The chain lever is close to used up.

Late share by lead at the win: under 6 h 15%, 6-12 h 9%, 24-36 h 7-8%, over 48 h 4.7%. Late cars spread over every pickup day.
